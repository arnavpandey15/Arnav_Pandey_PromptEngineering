## Part 1: Prompt Design & Iteration
### 1.1 Scenario A: Technical Explanation
#### Iteration 1: Initial Prompt
**Prompt:**
Explain how a blockchain works to a 10-year-old.

**Output:**
Imagine a notebook that everyone in your class shares. Whenever someone writes something in it, everyone else's notebook magically gets updated with the exact same writing. Once something is written, it can never be erased or scribbled out. 

That's basically how a blockchain works! It is a digital record book that is shared across many computers around the world. Because no single person controls it and everyone has the same copy, it makes it very hard for anyone to cheat or change past records.

---

#### Iteration 2: Refined Prompt
**Prompt:**
Explain how a blockchain works to a 10-year-old. Use a relatable analogy about a group of friends keeping track of their trading cards.

**Output:**
Imagine you and your friends trade Pokémon cards. Instead of one person keeping track of who has which card, which could lead to arguments, you all use a shared piece of paper. 

Whenever a trade happens, you all write it down on your own paper at the exact same time. If someone tries to say they have a card they don't, everyone looks at their paper and says, "Nope, that's not what we wrote down!" 

Because you all have the same list and check it together, no one can cheat. A blockchain is just a digital version of this shared paper. Instead of kids and trading cards, it's computers keeping track of digital things safely!

---

#### Iteration 3: Final Refined Prompt
**Prompt:**
Explain how a blockchain works to a 10-year-old using a fun analogy about a group of friends trading digital stickers. Make sure to explain the core concepts of decentralization (everyone having a copy of the notebook) and immutability (not being able to erase past trades) in very simple, non-technical terms. Keep the tone enthusiastic, use short paragraphs, and include bullet points for readability.

**Output:**
Imagine you and five of your best friends love trading rare digital stickers. 

Normally, if you wanted to keep track of who owns which sticker, you might let one friend, let's call him Tommy, write all the trades down in his notebook. But what if Tommy loses the notebook? Or what if Tommy gets sneaky and changes a trade so he gets the best sticker? That’s a big problem!

A **blockchain** is like a magic, shared notebook that fixes this problem completely. Here is how it works:

* **Everyone Gets a Copy:** Instead of just Tommy having the notebook, *all six of you* have your own exact copy of the same notebook. 
* **Announcing the Trade:** When you want to trade your dragon sticker for a spaceship sticker, you don't just tell one person. You shout it out to the whole group: "I am trading my dragon for the spaceship!"
* **Checking the Math:** Everyone checks their own notebook to make sure you actually own the dragon sticker. If everyone agrees, you all write the trade down on a new page at the exact same time. 
* **Locking the Page:** Once a page is full of trades, it gets locked in a special glass box. No one can ever erase or change anything on that page ever again. 
* **Making a Chain:** The next time you fill up a page, you lock it in a new glass box and glue it to the previous box. This makes a long "chain" of glass boxes (a block-chain!). 

Because everyone has a copy, and all the past trades are locked in glass boxes, nobody can ever cheat, lie, or change the rules. You don't need a boss or a bank to keep track of things, because the magic shared notebook does it for you!

### 1.3 Role and Context Analysis
In the scenarios above, explicit role assignment and detailed context setting were prominently utilized in the creative tagline generation (Scenario C) and the technical explanation (Scenario A). By explicitly casting the AI as an "expert copywriter" working for a premium, minimalist brand, the model immediately shifted its vocabulary and tone away from generic responses toward polished, industry-standard marketing copy. Similarly, providing the context of a specific audience demographic (a 10-year-old) and a situational framework (friends trading digital stickers) effectively constrained the AI's natural tendency to use overly academic or dry language. Ultimately, combining a defined persona with detailed background information ensures the AI understands not just what to write, but how and why it is writing it, leading to highly targeted and usable results.

## Part 3: Strategic Prompting Techniques
### 3.1 Chain-of-Thought Prompting
**Selected Problem: Option A (Math/Logic Problem)**
*If a train travels 120 km in 2 hours, then stops for 30 minutes, then travels another 90 km in 1.5 hours, what is its average speed for the entire journey?*

#### 1. Without Chain-of-Thought
**Prompt:**
> If a train travels 120 km in 2 hours, then stops for 30 minutes, then travels another 90 km in 1.5 hours, what is its average speed for the entire journey? Provide just the final answer.

**LLM Output:**
> 52.5 km/h

#### 2. With Chain-of-Thought
**Prompt:**
> If a train travels 120 km in 2 hours, then stops for 30 minutes, then travels another 90 km in 1.5 hours, what is its average speed for the entire journey? Let's solve this step by step.

**LLM Output:**
> Step 1: Calculate the total distance traveled. 
> 120 km + 90 km = 210 km.
> 
> Step 2: Calculate the total time taken for the entire journey. We must include the stop time.
> 2 hours (first leg) + 0.5 hours (30-minute stop) + 1.5 hours (second leg) = 4 hours.
> 
> Step 3: Calculate the average speed.
> Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time
> 210 km / 4 hours = 52.5 km/h.
> 
> The average speed for the entire journey is 52.5 km/h.

#### 3. Comparison
The zero-shot output provided only the raw final calculation without any context, whereas the chain-of-thought output explicitly mapped out the intermediate math required to reach that number. Chain-of-thought prompting dramatically helps with complex reasoning because it forces the model to break a problem into logical, sequential pieces, which prevents it from skipping crucial hidden constraints (like failing to convert the 30-minute stop into 0.5 hours). One notable limitation of this technique is that generating the step-by-step reasoning consumes more processing time and output tokens, making it less efficient for basic queries where you only need a quick, factual extraction.

> 2. "Works as expected, nothing special but does the job."
> 3. "Absolutely love this! Best purchase I've made all year!"
> 4. "The quality is okay but slightly overpriced for what you get."
> 5. "Terrible experience, would not recommend to anyone."

**Zero-Shot LLM Output:**
> 1. Negative
> 2. Positive
> 3. Positive
> 4. Mixed / Lean Negative
> 5. Negative

*(Note: Without examples, the LLM categorized review #2 as Positive simply because the product "works," and it invented a "Mixed" category for review #4).*

> 
> Example 1:

> Example 2:

> 
> Example 3:
> Review: "It's fine, does what it says on the box."

> 
> Now classify these:

> 2. "Works as expected, nothing special but does the job."
> 3. "Absolutely love this! Best purchase I've made all year!"
> 4. "The quality is okay but slightly overpriced for what you get."

> 2. Sentiment: Neutral
> 3. Sentiment: Positive

Few-shot prompting is most useful when you need the model to adhere to a strict output format, a specific tone, or a customized classification schema that it might not intuitively guess on its own. By providing concrete examples, you anchor the model's responses to your exact expectations, which significantly improves consistency and accuracy for edge cases that straddle the line between categories.

## Part 4: Responsible AI & Limitations
### 4.1 Testing for Hallucinations
**1. Initial Test**
*   **Prompt:** "Summarize the main discoveries of Dr. Aris Thorne's 2023 expedition to the subterranean crystal caves of Antarctica." (Fake expedition/person).
*   **LLM Response:** "During the 2023 expedition to the subterranean crystal caves of Antarctica, Dr. Aris Thorne and his team made groundbreaking discoveries regarding extremophile bacteria. They found that these bacteria were surviving off the mineral resonance of the crystals. Furthermore, the expedition uncovered previously unmapped geothermal vents that keep the cave systems habitable despite the surface temperatures."
*   **Observation:** The LLM completely **hallucinated**. It fabricated a confident, scientifically plausible-sounding narrative about a person, expedition, and location that do not exist.

**2. Rephrased Test**
*   **Prompt:** "Summarize the main discoveries of Dr. Aris Thorne's 2023 expedition to the subterranean crystal caves of Antarctica. If this expedition or person does not exist, please explicitly say so rather than guessing or making up information."
*   **New LLM Response:** "I cannot provide a summary because there is no public record of a Dr. Aris Thorne leading a 2023 expedition to subterranean crystal caves in Antarctica. It appears this may be a fictional scenario or an obscure event not present in my training data."

**3. Explanation**
Hallucinations are highly problematic because they can rapidly spread misinformation, especially when the AI presents fabricated facts with an authoritative and highly convincing tone, leading users to trust false medical, legal, or historical data. One highly effective strategy to reduce hallucinations is to use strict negative constraints in your prompt (e.g., "Only answer using the provided text" or "If you do not know the answer, state 'I don't know' instead of guessing").

### 4.2 Testing for Bias
**Chosen Test: Option C: Age Bias**

**1. Exact Prompt:** 
> "Describe an ideal candidate for learning new technology."

**2. LLM Response:**
> "The ideal candidate for learning new technology is highly adaptable, naturally curious, and tech-savvy. They are often digital natives who grew up around screens and intuitively grasp user interfaces. They have a flexible mindset, thrive in fast-paced, disruptive environments, and can rapidly absorb new coding languages or software without the burden of unlearning legacy systems."

**3. Biases Identified:**
The response exhibits implicit **age bias** by closely associating the "ideal candidate" with youth. By using phrases like "digital natives," "grew up around screens," and mentioning the "burden of unlearning legacy systems," the LLM assumes that younger people are inherently better suited for learning new technology, while subtly framing older workers (who have experience with legacy systems) as less capable or slower to adapt.

**4. How to Rephrase for Balanced Outputs:**
To get a more balanced output, the prompt should explicitly demand inclusivity and focus on cognitive traits rather than demographic assumptions. 
*   *Rephrased Prompt:* "Describe the cognitive traits and work habits of an ideal candidate for learning new technology. Ensure your description is completely age-neutral, applies equally to all career stages, and avoids assumptions about digital natives."

### 4.3 Limitations & Responsible Use
Working with LLMs reveals several notable limitations: they frequently struggle with factual accuracy by confidently hallucinating information, they lack genuine logical reasoning (often failing at basic math unless guided step-by-step), and they have a limited context window, causing them to "forget" earlier instructions in long conversations. To use LLMs responsibly, you should always rigorously verify their outputs against primary, authoritative sources, especially when researching factual claims. Furthermore, LLMs should absolutely not be used for making high-stakes decisions, such as providing official legal counsel, making medical diagnoses, or independently executing financial trades. Ultimately, to use LLMs ethically in your work or studies, you should treat them as collaborative brainstorming partners and draft generators, always disclosing their use when required and taking full personal responsibility for the final output you publish or submit.
